definition: "<p>Landing page design is the practice of building a single page around one conversion goal - a signup, a purchase, a demo request - and removing everything that doesn't directly serve that goal. Unlike a normal website page, which usually has to serve multiple audiences and multiple paths, a landing page exists for exactly one visitor intent, and every design decision gets judged against whether it helps or distracts from that one action.</p>"

why_it_matters: "<p>Most landing pages fail not because the offer is weak but because the page is asking the visitor to make several decisions at once - read this, also consider that, maybe click here instead. A visitor arriving from a specific ad or campaign already has a narrow intent; a page that broadens their options at that moment usually loses them rather than converting them.</p><p>The design discipline here is closer to subtraction than addition. Standard site navigation, secondary offers, and unrelated content all reduce the odds of the one action the page exists for - so the hardest design decisions on a landing page are usually about what to leave out, not what to add.</p>"
