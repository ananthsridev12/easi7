SMS gets read within minutes, which makes it the right channel for a narrow set of messages - not a replacement for email. We use it for what it's actually good at.

* Order and delivery status updates that reduce support tickets
* Abandoned-cart recovery sequences timed for the first hour after drop-off
* Time-sensitive offers and limited-window promotions
* Opt-in and consent management that keeps you compliant, not just effective
* Frequency capping so SMS stays a high-open-rate channel instead of an unsubscribe trigger

### The rule we don't break

If a message isn't genuinely time-sensitive or transactional, it doesn't go out as SMS. That discipline is why the channel still works when a lot of lists have been burned by over-sending.
