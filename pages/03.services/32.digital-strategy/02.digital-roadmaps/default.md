A roadmap that lists everything as high priority isn't a roadmap - it's a wishlist with a timeline attached. The actual work of roadmapping is deciding what genuinely has to happen before something else can, and being honest about what a team can carry in parallel without everything slowing down at once.

We start by inventorying every initiative already in motion or proposed, often surfacing duplicate or conflicting work nobody had connected yet. From there, dependencies get mapped, effort gets scored against real capacity, and the sequence gets built around what the map actually shows - not around which stakeholder pushed hardest for their initiative to go first.
